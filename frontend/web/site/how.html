How it works

OpenElo rates teams from their results, then every player from his team's rating and what he adds himself. It uses nothing but match results and minutes played, and anyone can recompute it.

From results to a player's number

  1. Rate the teams

    After every match both teams' ratings move by how surprising the result was: beating a stronger side earns more than beating a weaker one, and a bigger margin counts a little more. Clubs from different leagues meet in European competitions, which puts every league on one scale.

  2. Start each player from his club

    A player's rating begins with his club's current rating. Move to a stronger club and that part rises the moment he plays for it.

  3. Add how often he plays

    Over his last 50 matchday squads: a start counts as a full match, a substitute appearance as his minutes, an unused substitute as nothing. Compared with his club's 16 most-used players, each share point is worth 4 rating points (400 × (his share − the club's)). Goalkeepers are measured against playing every match.

  4. Add goals and assists

    100 points per goal or assist per 90 minutes, over the same squads, with 5 scoreless matches added so a few lucky minutes can't carry it.

  5. Add national-team selection

    Being picked by a strong national team adds points; not being picked never takes any away.

  6. Subtract recent absence

    Each match missed while at the club costs a little, up to a cap, and it comes back as he plays again. A player out of every squad for most of a year slides towards a replacement level.

Check it yourself

The formula, its constants and the code that computes it are public, in the openelo-player-market repository. Every published rating carries the version of the formula that produced it. A version is never changed after it is published: improvements arrive as a new version, announced in advance.

What OpenElo never uses: scouts' opinions, fan votes, transfer fees, market values, betting odds or trading on this site.

Questions

Why is a squad player rated far below his team-mates?

Because he rarely starts. The selection part compares how often he plays with his club's regulars; a player who mostly sits on the bench can be 200 points or more below the club.

Why did my player drop when he was injured?

Missed matches cost a little each, and his club may have lost without him. Both recover as he plays again.

Two players always play together. How are they told apart?

Only by what separates them: one missing a match, a different role for their country, a transfer. If nothing ever separates them, their ratings move together, and that is the honest answer.

Is a rating a valuation?

No. It measures how a player's teams do and how much he plays, scores and gets picked. It is not a transfer fee or a market value.
